Abstract

Modern 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis approaches have demonstrated strong performance on scenes with opaque, non-refractive objects. However, most assume straight light paths and therefore cannot properly handle refractive and reflective materials. The lack of datasets specialized for these effects has impeded efforts to fairly and thoroughly evaluate performance and thereby make progress in this domain. Most existing datasets focus on opaque scenes, while those targeting refractive objects are often limited to thin glass with negligible ray bending, untinted single-material objects, or backgrounds treated as infinitely distant, leaving key failure modes under strong refraction untested. To address this gap, we introduce the RefRef dataset. It contains 150 synthetic and 60 real scenes spanning varying levels of geometric complexity and diverse background types that expose the limitations of existing methods. For the purpose of benchmarking, we also provide an oracle method that, given the object geometry and refractive indices, calculates accurate light paths for neural rendering, and a simple two-stage baseline that relaxes these assumptions. We evaluate these against state-of-the-art methods and show that the task is far from solved.

Unlike existing datasets, RefRef contains objects with large refractive volumes, both clear and tinted materials, single and multi-material objects, and backgrounds from nearby geometry to distant environments. Across the scenes these appear in different combinations, producing strong light bending, multiple refractions, total internal reflection and light absorption.

Backgrounds and environments

  • Bounded (100 synthetic): a cube or sphere with high-frequency textures at a finite distance, so the observed color depends on both the origin and direction of the refracted ray.
  • Unbounded (50 synthetic): HDR environment maps treated as infinitely far away.
  • Indoor (30 real): a living room lit by ceiling lights and a darker puzzle table lit by a lamp.
  • Outdoor (30 real): direct sunlight with static shadows in a courtyard, and after sunset on a lookout deck.

Benchmark Reference Methods

Oracle

Given the ground-truth object geometry and refractive indices, the oracle traces physically accurate light paths. Samples along each camera ray are updated at every surface intersection, and refracted and reflected rays are handled separately. A Zip-NeRF field predicts color and density for each sample, and the rendered colors are combined with the Fresnel equations. The oracle models refraction, reflection, total internal reflection and absorption.

R3F: a simple two-stage baseline

R3F (Refractive–Reflective Radiance Field) removes the need for ground-truth geometry and refractive indices. It first estimates the object's geometry and refractive index with NU-NeRF, then fits a Zip-NeRF background field with the object masked out (masks predicted by SAM 3). Finally, it fits a separate in-object field using the oracle's ray tracing while the background field stays frozen, which prevents floaters outside the object. Its performance depends entirely on the quality of the estimated geometry.

Quantitative Results
